Companies on Ivanti VPNs: attackers chained three zero-days. Here is how the attack worked, and why one patch was not enough.

Ivanti Connect Secure is an SSL VPN. It is the box that lets employees reach the company network from home, so it sits right on the internet.

Researchers at Volexity say the attacks began on October third, against an appliance that was fully patched. On October thirteenth, Ivanti disclosed and fixed the first flaw. On October twenty-ninth, it patched two more. And on October thirtieth, the US cyber agency CISA added them to its list of known exploited bugs.

The chain had three steps. First, bypass the login check. Then, run code through a file upload race. Finally, escalate to root, full control of the device, without ever logging in.

Once inside, the attackers took stored credentials, then searched Active Directory for local admin passwords and BitLocker recovery keys, the keys that unlock encrypted laptops.

And it was hard to spot. No web shell was left behind, logs were edited, and code was changed only in memory. Ivanti's own Integrity Checker Tool missed it.

Here is the catch. If you patched on October thirteenth, you are not done. Those versions are still open to the two newer flaws. The fixed versions are on screen.

CISA gave US federal agencies until November second to fix the root flaw and check for compromise, and until November thirteenth for the third one.

Analyst note: your VPN is the front door, and attackers keep testing it. Patch it like an emergency, and watch it with your own network monitoring, because a clean scan is not proof.

What to do this week. Upgrade every Connect Secure appliance to a fixed release. Don't trust a clean Integrity Checker scan on its own. Preserve memory and logs before you reboot or reset. And if you suspect compromise, rotate the exposed credentials.
