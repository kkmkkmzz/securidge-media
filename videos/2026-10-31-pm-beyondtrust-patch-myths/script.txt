IT teams: patching this bug was not enough. Here are three myths about the BeyondTrust zero-day, and the facts that bust them.

BeyondTrust Remote Support is the software help desks use to take control of a computer and fix it. A flaw in it let remote attackers skip the login entirely.

BeyondTrust rates it nine point three out of ten, critical. It affects Remote Support version twenty-six point two point three and earlier.

Myth one: if a bug is not public yet, nobody is using it.

Fact: the first known compromises date to August twenty-ninth. BeyondTrust contained one on September twenty-eighth, patched its cloud customers by October ninth, went public on the fifteenth, and the US agency CISA flagged it as exploited on the sixteenth.

Myth two: once you patch, you are safe.

Fact: as Rapid7 puts it, patching does not remove persistence. Anything the attackers left behind is still there.

Mandiant, part of Google, says the attackers planted a web shell, took root control, tried to pull credentials from the vault, and set up ways to stay in. It links them to a suspected China-nexus espionage group it calls Armory Castle.

Myth three: no alerts means no intruders.

Fact: the attackers deleted logs. BeyondTrust itself warns: an absence of known indicators does not establish that an appliance is uncompromised.

Analyst note: the tools that hold the keys are the best front door. Limit who can reach them, and keep their logs somewhere attackers cannot erase.

If your company runs it, do this today. Patch self-hosted servers. Hunt for signs of compromise, even after patching. Rotate the passwords it stores. And send its logs to an outside system.
