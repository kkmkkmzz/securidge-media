Android work phones, So-net customers, and a criminal web host. Here is your Week in Security for October thirtieth.

Story one. A flaw in Microsoft Authenticator for Android let a malicious app pretend to be Microsoft Teams, and collect access tokens for your work account.

Microsoft scored it eight point six, rated Important, and shipped the fix in September. Microsoft says it has not been exploited.

In Enclave's tests, those tokens opened mail, files and calendars. Microsoft says an attack needs the user to complete a sign-in step in Authenticator.

Story two. Sony Network Communications says attackers broke into the member database of its internet provider So-net, exposing more than two point five eight million customer records.

The break-in happened on October nineteenth. The company shut the server on the twenty-third, confirmed the leak on the twenty-fourth, and went public on the twenty-seventh.

Names, phone numbers and dates of birth were exposed, plus fifty-four thousand four hundred and one email passwords. The company has reset those passwords.

Story three. On October twenty-seventh, the FBI seized the domain of Virtualine, a so-called bulletproof hosting provider, working with police in Germany, the Netherlands and the UK.

Prosecutors say it kept a criminal forum online after police complaints, and laundered money. It even boasted that it ignored more abuse than your last three therapists.

Analyst note: the common thread this week is that attackers want what already lets you in. Tokens, passwords, and hosting that looks trusted. Multi-factor authentication helps most on an updated device.

Do this today. Update Microsoft Authenticator on Android. Install apps only from trusted stores. If you are a So-net customer, set a new email password. And treat any message about the breach as suspicious.
