# Sources - "Hi Mum" / new-number family impersonation scam

Primary
- Scamwatch (ACCC, Australian Competition and Consumer Commission), "ACCC warning of suspicious messages as 'Hi Mum' scams spike" (Aug 23, 2022): https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/node/1065 - scammer poses as family member, usually on WhatsApp, says phone lost/damaged and writing from a new number; builds rapport then asks for money for an urgent bill, claiming no temporary access to online banking; 82% of family impersonation scams reported by people over 55 (first seven months of 2022); advice: call the number already saved, ask a personal question, "never send money without being absolutely sure who you are sending it to."
- FBI IC3 PSA I-120324-PSA (Dec 3, 2024): https://www.ic3.gov/psa/2024/psa241203 - background on relative-impersonation in a crisis; advice to hang up and call the number directly (background only, not quoted on screen).

Secondary (reporting a police warning)
- Deeside.com (Apr 20, 2026), North Wales Police Cyber Crime unit: https://www.deeside.com/new-twist-on-hi-mum-scam-sees-criminals-impersonating-parents-in-text-messages/ - new twist: texts appearing to come from a parent who lost/forgot their bank card, asking the recipient to cover a bill; rise in reports; advice: "Do not reply directly in the message stream", contact relative on saved number, forward suspicious texts to 7726, contact bank if money sent.
