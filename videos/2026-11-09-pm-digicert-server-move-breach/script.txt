DigiCert customers had data and some ID documents exposed. One server move left a door open for more than a year. Here is how it happened.

DigiCert is a certificate authority. It issues the digital certificates that browsers use to trust websites. This time, its business customers were the ones affected.

In February twenty twenty-five, an internal system was moved, and it became reachable from the internet. On June eighteenth this year, staff spotted strange outside accounts, but judged them harmless. From June twenty-seventh to July second, an unauthorized account read archived customer data. DigiCert only confirmed that data access on October nineteenth.

Root cause number one: the move skipped a security review. DigiCert says it was treated as an infrastructure change, not a security change.

Root cause number two: a self-registration feature could be reached from outside. Outsiders could create their own accounts, with no multi-factor authentication.

Root cause number three: those new accounts had no app assigned, yet they could still read the archive. And no alert fired when strangers signed up.

That is about sixteen months between the server move and the first warning sign.

What was exposed? Mostly names, business emails, phone numbers and business addresses. For a small minority, identity documents too, like passports, driving licences or utility bills.

DigiCert has not said how many customers were affected. It reported the incident to law enforcement, and says it has no evidence the information was misused.

The good news: in DigiCert's words, no certificate operations were impacted. Certificates and logins were not affected, so customers do not need to replace their certificates.

Analyst note: network walls are not permissions. If a system is safe only because it is hidden, one move can expose it.

Four lessons. Treat every network change as a security change. Check permissions in the app, not just the network. Alert on new accounts and unusual traffic. And if you are a customer, watch for phishing that uses your business details.
