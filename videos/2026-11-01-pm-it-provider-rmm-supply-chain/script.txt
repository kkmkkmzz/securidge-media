Small businesses: your IT provider could be the way in. Here is how one hacked management tool can reach every computer it looks after.

Many IT providers use remote monitoring and management tools, called RMM. From one console they deploy software, run scripts and open remote sessions on computers across many companies.

A real example is N-able N-central. In September, N-able fixed CVE-2026-86218, a critical flaw that let attackers run code on the N-central server without logging in.

On September fourth, the security firm Huntress investigated a break-in at one customer's N-central server. On the fifth, N-able shipped Hotfix 4. On the eighth, the US agency CISA listed the flaw as exploited, and gave federal agencies until the eleventh to patch.

Huntress says limited logging means it cannot confirm which flaw was used in that break-in. N-able later told customers the flaw has been exploited in the wild.

Researchers at watchTowr, who reproduced the flaw, put it simply: compromise N-central, and you gain access to all connected computers and downstream systems.

And it was not a one-off. Between August second and September fifth, N-able patched five N-central security flaws across four hotfixes.

If your company runs N-central on its own servers, update to 2026.3 Hotfix 4 or later. N-able says its hosted customers were already patched.

Analyst note: your security is partly your provider's security. The tool that can touch every computer deserves the strongest locks, and you are allowed to ask about them.

Four questions for your IT provider. Is your management tool patched, and how fast? Is its login hidden from the open internet? Would you notice a new admin account? And do you review its logs and remote sessions?
